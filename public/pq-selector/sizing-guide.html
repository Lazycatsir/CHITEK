Step 1

Check Power Quality First

Before installing an AHF, you need to understand the actual system problem. The most useful first step is a 24-48 hour power quality measurement on the main feeder or the dominant nonlinear load group.

Harmonics

Use a power quality analyzer and watch Total Harmonic Distortion values. A THD level above 5% is already a sign that harmonic mitigation should be reviewed.

Voltage changes

Check whether the system voltage fluctuates more than ±5% from normal operating conditions.

Operational symptoms

Frequent circuit breaker trips, overheated transformers, or cable overheating usually indicate deeper power quality issues.

Example

A factory measured 12% THD on the production line, which was contributing to motor overheating and thermal stress.

Step 2A

AHF Sizing Math

AHF sizing is based on harmonic current, not just on total load current. The key goal is to calculate how much distorted current the filter must cancel.

Formula 1: Harmonic current

Harmonic Current = Total Current × THDi

If a feeder draws 500A total current and THDi is 20%, the harmonic current is:

500 × 0.20 = 100A

Formula 2: Safety margin

Recommended AHF = Harmonic Current × 1.2 to 1.3

For a 100A harmonic current, a practical recommendation is about 120A to 130A.

Choose 130A if future expansion is expected.

Voltage match

AHF Voltage Rating = System Voltage

Always align the AHF voltage class with the site voltage, such as 220V, 400V, 440V, or 480V.

Extra margin rule

+10% for dynamic converter loads

For solar inverters, variable-speed drives, or other rapidly changing loads, add extra capacity for transient operating conditions.

Practical Rule

AHF size should be large enough to handle present harmonic current and still leave room for future development.

Step 2B

SVG Sizing Math

SVG sizing is based on reactive power correction. The purpose is to close the gap between the present power factor and the target power factor.

Formula 1: Reactive compensation

Qc = P × (tan arccos PF1 − tan arccos PF2)

Where P is active power in kW, PF1 is current power factor, and PF2 is target power factor.

Example

If a site runs at 800 kW, with PF improving from 0.78 to 0.98:

Qc ≈ 800 × (tan arccos 0.78 − tan arccos 0.98)

This gives the approximate kvar rating needed from the SVG.

If only kVA is known

P ≈ kVA × PF

When no kW data is available, active power can be approximated from transformer or feeder kVA and existing power factor.

Margin guidance

Recommended SVG = Calculated kvar × 1.05 to 1.15

Apply a practical design margin for system changes and fluctuating load demand.

Step 3

Set Up Wiring & Controls

Power connections

Use copper cables sized to match the unit current. Cable routing, ventilation, and breaker sizing should follow local electrical standards.

Control wiring

Connect communication cables such as RS485 where monitoring or external supervision is required.

Recommended initial setting

Set harmonic compensation to Auto mode during startup unless the project requires a custom compensation profile.

CT Placement

Where to Place Current Sensors

Option 1: Load-side (open-loop)

Place CTs close to noisy equipment such as VFD groups. This is useful when you want to target specific machines or process lines.

Option 2: Grid-side (closed-loop)

Install CTs at the main incoming power point. This protects the full system, though it may be less focused on smaller branch issues.

Multiple AHF systems

For parallel units, CT strategy should be coordinated. Shared CTs or extra output CTs may be needed for accurate compensation control.

Step 4

Install & Test

Mounting

Install the unit in a cool, dry location with at least 30 cm of ventilation clearance around the equipment.

Power-on check

Close the breaker, confirm normal startup, and verify that there are no alarm or error indicators.

Performance check

Compare harmonic levels before and after startup with a clamp meter or power quality analyzer. Run for 24 hours and confirm ambient temperature stays below 55°C.

Safety first

Use insulated gloves and follow site lockout and energization procedures when working around live equipment.

Step 5

Monitor Results

Daily checks

Review the display for compensation current, operating temperature, and any error or warning codes.

Weekly reports

Track THDi trends and verify whether the system remains below the target threshold.

Rapid troubleshooting

If THDi rises again above 5%, inspect nearby equipment and confirm whether new nonlinear loads have been added.

Why This Matters

Engineering Value of Correct Sizing

Lower utility penalties

Improved power quality and power factor can reduce avoidable electricity charges.

Longer equipment life

Cleaner power reduces overheating stress on motors, transformers, and sensitive equipment.

System protection

Good compensation can help prevent nuisance trips and damage caused by harmonic and reactive power issues.

Advanced operating mode

Modern systems can automatically switch between harmonic compensation, reactive power correction, and load balancing depending on the site condition.

Technical Reminder

For higher-performance applications, the system should cover at least up to the 50th harmonic, approximately 2.5 kHz.
